🫖 Traditional Chinese Gongfu Brewing

The ancient art — many small infusions, full flavor

What is Gongfu Brewing?

Gongfu cha means "making tea with skill." It uses more leaves, less water, and many short infusions. This method reveals every layer of flavor — each cup tastes slightly different. Best for oolong, pu-erh, and high-quality loose teas.

What You'll Need

  • Gaiwan — Lidded bowl (traditional & versatile)
  • Yixing Teapot — Unglazed clay — gets better with age
  • Fairness Pitcher — To pour evenly into cups
  • Small Cups — 30–50ml each
  • Hot Water Kettle

Step-by-Step Method

  1. Warm the vessel — Pour hot water into your gaiwan or teapot, swirl it around, then pour out. This keeps the tea hot longer.
  2. Add leaves — Fill about 1/3 to 1/2 full of dry tea leaves. More than usual!
  3. Rinse the leaves — Pour hot water over the leaves, then pour it out immediately (5–10 seconds only). This "wakes up" the leaves. Don't drink this first rinse!
  4. First infusion — Add hot water, put the lid on, wait 20–30 seconds, then pour everything out completely into the pitcher. Don't leave water sitting on the leaves!
  5. Serve & enjoy — Pour from the pitcher into small cups. Smell first, then sip slowly.
  6. Re-steep — Add 10–15 seconds to the time for each subsequent infusion. Good oolong and pu-erh can go 8–15+ times!

Timing Guide

Infusion # Time
1st (after rinse) 20 – 30 seconds
2nd – 4th 30 – 45 seconds
5th – 8th 45 – 60 seconds
9th+ +30 seconds each time

Tip: Gongfu brewing is a conversation with your tea. Pay attention — if it's too strong, shorten the time. Too weak? Steep longer next time.
