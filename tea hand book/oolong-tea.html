🍃 Oolong Tea Profiles

Partially oxidized — between green and black tea

What is Oolong Tea?

Oolong tea is partially oxidized, falling somewhere between green and black tea in flavor and color. Oxidation levels range from 15% to 70%, creating a wide variety of tastes — from floral and sweet to rich and toasty.

Popular Oolong Tea Varieties

  • Tie Guan Yin (Iron Goddess) — Floral, fragrant, smooth — one of China's most famous teas
  • Da Hong Pao (Big Red Robe) — Roasted, mineral, rich — a legendary rock tea
  • Dong Ding — Taiwanese, lightly roasted, nutty and sweet
  • High Mountain / Ali Shan — Taiwan, high altitude, creamy, buttery, floral
  • Bai Hao / Oriental Beauty — Heavily oxidized, honey-sweet, fruity, unique aroma
  • Phoenix Dancong — Complex floral aromas — honey, orchid, citrus notes

How to Brew Oolong Tea

Water Temp Steeping Time Leaf Amount
85°C – 95°C 2 – 4 minutes 1–2 tsp per cup

Tip: Oolong leaves can be re-steeped multiple times! Each infusion brings out different flavors.
